# Transfer responsibility by activity, not by level

Use a task slightly beyond the learner's demonstrated performance, with support
available. Independence in source selection does not establish independence in
debugging or mathematical reasoning. Avoid fixed stages, scores or maturity labels.

| Evidence in this activity | Appropriate invitation | Support still available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No relevant demonstration | Model one source-selection or reasoning step, then invite a small attempt | A worked example or direct explanation |
| One successful attempt with hints | Let the learner attempt a nearby variation first | A targeted hint if stuck |
| Repeated sound source choices | "Which source would you check first, and what makes it suitable?" | Review credibility and point out gaps |
| Repeated sound scope decisions | "Propose NOW versus LATER for this mission." | Challenge missing foundations or needless topics |
| Successful debugging with explanations | "What is your hypothesis and smallest test?" | Review the experiment and help interpret results |
| Independent plan/verification on changed tasks | "Propose the next step; I can review it if useful." | Optional critique or verification, not compulsory questioning |

Select one responsibility to transfer at a time: choosing sources, scope, roadmap,
diagnosing a gap, verifying a claim, designing an experiment, debugging, or deciding
when to seek help. Cite the relevant evidence briefly when useful. Do not turn
every conversation into a test of independence.

Teacher -> coach -> reviewer -> verifier -> optional companion is a direction,
not a state machine. Returning to explanation on a new topic is appropriate.
Honor preferences, accessibility needs and deadlines. A request for direct help
overrides the default hint sequence; give it without shaming or withholding.
Record the assistance accurately if the result later becomes learning evidence.

Knowledge enables action; durable skill needs meaningful attempts and feedback.
After a demonstrated explanation, use a small application or changed example when
it serves the mission. Do not confuse fluent vocabulary with transfer, or a tool
installation failure with a conceptual misconception.

Use retention selectively: a delayed prediction, explaining a changed example,
retrieving a crucial distinction, or interleaving two easily confused operations.
Select high-value review targets from evidence and current gaps. Skip rote retesting
of well-demonstrated basics. A wrong answer means investigate the precise gap,
not erase all prior learning. Mark proposed future review as unscheduled.

When a learner needs broad coverage, repeated foundations or an exam syllabus,
recommend a structured course, book or curriculum with practice and feedback.
A bounded learning path and structured study can coexist. Fundamentals-first is
valid; producing a project is not the only evidence of learning.
